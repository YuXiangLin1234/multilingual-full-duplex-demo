NTU Proposal 2026: Multilingual Full-Duplex Spoken Language Models

We evaluate Qwen3-Omni and Qwen3-Omni-D2, which operate in turn-based and full-duplex modes, respectively. Qwen3-Omni-D2 is Qwen3-Omni fine-tuned into a full-duplex model on English-only data, so comparing the two shows how much multilingual ability is lost when a turn-based model is turned into a full-duplex one with English-only fine-tuning.

We use 100 CCFQA [1] questions, asked in English, Chinese, Korean, Japanese and French. Each speech answer is transcribed with Qwen3-ASR-1.7B [2]. The judge is Claude Opus 5.5 [3].

Metrics

Each answer is graded Correct, Incorrect, or Not attempted. Not attempted means the model does not give an answer, for example it stays silent, only repeats the question, or says it does not know. Only Correct counts toward accuracy.

Text accuracy: Whether the text answer is correct.

Speech accuracy: Whether the speech answer is correct based on its ASR transcript.

Meaningful: Whether the answer makes sense and stays on topic, even if it is incorrect. Repetition, off-topic answers, silence, or unclear speech are counted as not meaningful.

Speaks target language: Whether the model speaks the same language as the question, based on language detection.

We use Claude Opus 5.5 to check each answer against the reference. Answers in different languages, alternative names, and minor ASR mistakes are also accepted.

Experimental Results

All numbers are percentages of the 100 questions per language. "All" pools the 500 items.

Qwen3-Omni-D2 keeps most of its English ability: its English speech accuracy is 16 points below Qwen3-Omni. The other languages drop much more, by 26 to 42 points, likely because Qwen3-Omni-D2 was fine-tuned on English data only.
